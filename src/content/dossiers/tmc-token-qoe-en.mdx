## What breaks

Generative AI answers are produced token by token, so a user feels two delays: the time to the first token (TTFT) and the time per output token (TPOT). Most offloading work treats a request as a bit-stream and a local-or-remote choice. That misses how prompt length drives both delays, and how much of a long image or video prompt is redundant.

In a remote area with no base stations the choice is real. A small model on a drone answers fast at lower quality. A full foundation model on a LEO satellite answers better and later. And a drone can cache only a few models.

## The idea

Treat everything as tokens. One continuous ratio η compresses the whole input sequence, so it sets the uplink payload and the prefill work together, and therefore TTFT. It also costs quality, and the scheduler can price that cost.

<Eq tex="\tilde n_u^{\rm in}=\eta_u\,(n_u^{\rm txt}+n_u^{\rm vis}),\qquad \eta_u\in[\eta_{\min},1]" note="One ratio compresses the input. It sets both the payload on the link and the work in prefill." />

The first token has to cross the whole chain in series. Later tokens stream through a pipeline, so they are limited by its slowest stage.

<Eq tex="\mathrm{TTFT}_u=T^{\rm G2A}_{u\to k}+T^{\rm A2G}_{k\to u}+(1-x_u)\mathrm{TTFT}^{\rm UAV}_u+x_u\left[T^{\rm A2S}+T^{{\rm ISL},\uparrow}+\mathrm{TTFT}^{\rm sat}_u+T^{{\rm ISL},\downarrow}+T^{\rm S2A}\right]" />

<Eq tex="\mathrm{TPOT}_u=\begin{cases}\max\{\overline{\mathrm{TPOT}}^{\rm UAV}_u,\;T^{\rm A2G}_{k\to u}\}&x_u=0\\ \max\{\overline{\mathrm{TPOT}}^{\rm sat}_u,\;T^{{\rm ISL},\downarrow},\;T^{\rm S2A},\;T^{\rm A2G}\}&x_u=1\end{cases}" note="The first-token delay is a serial sum. The per-token delay is a pipeline bottleneck." />

<Eq tex="\mathrm{QoE}_u=w_q\ln(1+Q_u)-w_1\,\mathrm{TTFT}_u-w_2\,\mathrm{TPOT}_u" note="A concave reward for quality, with diminishing returns, minus penalties for both delays." />

## The system

Three tiers and two timescales. Every 120 s a knowledge-driven planner proposes UAV positions and model caches from recent demand, scores each candidate by rolling out the fast scheduler, and refines by alternating search. Every 2.4 s slot a conditional-diffusion TD3 scheduler (CD-TD3) picks an offload tier and a compression ratio for each user, and closed-form rules allocate bandwidth and compute.

## What it found

The evaluation is a simulation: 5 UAVs, 30 users, 6 satellites and a library of 10 UAV models, with decode costs set from NVIDIA Jetson AGX Thor rates and quality coefficients from model cards. Results are over 30 held-out configurations. The proposed framework reaches the highest per-user QoE, **31.9% above TD3 and 69.2% above the Greedy heuristic**.

Where the gain comes from is visible in that table. Against the fixed-configuration variant the proposed scheme cuts TTFT from 1.225 s to 0.873 s. In the learned behaviour, 35.7% of requests with a cached model still go to the satellite voluntarily, and vision inputs headed there are compressed to about η = 0.44.

Removing one component at a time shows what carries the result. Per-user factorization matters most, then the diffusion actor, then compression.
