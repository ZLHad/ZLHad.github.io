## What breaks

Satellites, drones and ground stations share radio, compute and battery across layers that differ widely in delay and power. Classical optimization assumes a tidy static model. Deep reinforcement learning adapts, but it is a black box that cannot tell an intent for emergency reliability from one for best-effort capacity. Recent work with language models mostly asks for a one-off answer on a static snapshot, so nothing watches the result or corrects it.

## The idea

Divide the work by timescale. A language model reads the situation and rewrites the reward function of a fast reinforcement-learning agent. It does not make the fast decisions. LLM calls take a few seconds, well decoupled from an RL loop deciding at the millisecond level.

## Inside the Plan phase

Two mechanisms connect the orchestrator to the RL agent. Reward shaping turns a semantic condition into a modified reward: under energy constraints, penalize high-power transmissions and favor energy-efficient paths. Diffusion-based exploration uses historical trajectories in the knowledge base to guide what the agent tries.

## Keeping it safe

A language model in a control loop needs a guard. Every LLM output is checked against a JSON schema and parameter bounds, and the RL agents fall back to a safe default reward whenever the check fails. The learner updates prompts and rewards only when KPIs drift past a threshold or on a schedule, and each update carries a recorded reasoning trace.

## How it was tested

The paper validates the mechanism in a simulated UAV-assisted AIGC orchestration scenario under energy constraints: 3 LEO satellites, 5 UAVs, 2 ground base stations and 50 concurrent tasks, comparing six methods. The comparison is in the paper.
